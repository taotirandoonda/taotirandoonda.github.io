What’s Ours

Salvador, Bahia, November 2026

Written to Brazilians, the week after we won a runoff together. Translated as is.

Lula’s win was enormous. I think the tremendous collective effort behind it finally showed us who we are. At least it showed me, like never before. I found it beautiful and it left me inspired. Ten million on Janones’s beat. People in general, from fandoms to axé, artists and teachers, workers, everyone out in the streets. People talking to everybody, calling up friends from ten years ago. Standing up to big tech and the disinformation apparatus. And here in Salvador, we were left with half the buses parked in the garages and nobody waited for a leader to act: we organized on social media, split gas money, and whoever had a car drove strangers to the polls. What power we have.

The battles are many and at every scale: in our lives, our neighborhoods, cities, states, the country, the world. We can’t ignore any of them, and we can’t fight them all at once. But the war is one. We fight the battles in front of us, always in tune with the greater war. That war isn’t just the advance of authoritarianism, or some variant of it. Authoritarianism is just the armor: the protective layer over a power asymmetry that’s getting enormous. The war is to bring back balance. And with that, the threat of authoritarianism comes apart.

Our ancestors already went through this asymmetry. Language was the first collective technology we ever had. Nobody built it; evolution gave it to everyone. Imagine, millions of years of evolution, communicating with grunts and gestures until, finally, we could stop, take a step back, look at the whole thing and say: “Holy shit, what the fu...?!”

The moment it could be written down, a small group took control: scribes, priests, the Bible locked in Latin, people burned for translating it. The pattern is old: something that belongs to everyone shows up, and a few try to hold it. Power seduces.

And it’s happening again. In the last few years we got two new collective technologies: social networks, which connect us and help us coordinate, and AI, which synthesizes everything humanity has ever recorded. All our cultural richness and our ancestors’: art, science, music, math. The size of it! The generations upon generations involved, the accumulation and mixing of ideas over time, across the whole world. All our history, lived and recorded: captured. These technologies are ours, the people’s.

Around them, the armor gets stronger: military and police technologies, coercion, detention, surveillance. And inside, the power itself grows: control over innovation and knowledge, and economic automation. All of it moving at full speed, each part feeding the others. But the entirety of the machine is heavy and sprawling. Every step demands more land, more water, more energy, more minerals, more chips. It turns out the cloud sits on the ground, and using the ground needs our permission. Part of our leverage is our land. 71% of Americans already don’t want a data center in their neighborhood. And several countries around the world are already suffering as the machine sprawls.

We already let a lot slide. For instance, we knew Meta harvests our data. And a lot happened unnoticed: millions of books were scanned and then thrown away to train AI, which we only found out about through lawsuits, and billions of our photos were scraped by Clearview to serve police and ICE. It ended in fines, which for these people are simply the cost of doing business.

Who are we, against this enormous machine that only grows and gets stronger? PEOPLE! URGENT!! (Janones inside joke.) We’re the same ones who managed to unite and secure Lula's victory. Our coordination is another part of our leverage.

Our political and economic system let these technologies get built, and let them end up in the hands of a few. They ended up evolving much faster than our institutions could adapt.

The truth is, extraordinary things really did get created: everyone is connected and all our knowledge is synthesized. But a lot was taken to get there. And now the owners are betting on our collective acceptance that what’s done is done… and that they can keep going. I don’t know about you, but I don’t accept it. I feel disrespected, because they’re not even asking: they’re taking, stripping, and imposing. It’s an affront to our personal, national, and collective sovereignty. Strip away all the layers of theater and what’s left is nothing but a terrible deal, imposed without our consent.

Just like we united to vote a big NO to authoritarianism at the ballot box, we need to coordinate to vote another enormous NO to this deal. Everyone who’s fed up, in any country, can give that NO. For us, the battle in front of us was the vote, and it was what united us. The NO can come from anywhere, and it needs to come in many forms. The threat is present across the whole world. Imagine our strength, even more united.

Just because we haven’t managed to hit the brakes so far doesn’t mean we shouldn’t. We need to demand more justice in our courts and work to strengthen them, and we need to put those responsible on trial. The consequences can’t only be fines.

Now, notice that they still need us to buy their theater. Giving one reason or another, they try to justify the new policies, the “security” measures, the pacts, the data center in your backyard. They even interfere in elections! Because an election is when the collective will gets shown. All of this shows they still need our consent. Their theater is evidence of our leverage.

We can’t simply ignore the theater. For many of us, our salaries come from the game. And there are institutions and props, like money, that reinforce the theater. Pull the thread, and we find it’s violence that holds up the whole charade: war, police that kill, inhumane prisons, executions of prisoners. So anyone afraid of losing their job, or afraid of suffering violence, is not a coward. But, if you can fight, find your way to pitch in.

To those working for big tech: if you stay, and maybe you should, because otherwise you’ll just be replaced, then organize and refuse en masse. If someone takes a stand, back them up and give them support. If you can afford the risk, take it. If enough of you refuse to go on without changes, you force a negotiation. And if the company won’t negotiate, walk out together, and help each other on the outside.

We’ve already learned not to give nonsense a stage. And here that’s still true. Where we can, we shrug — we refuse the charade. When each of us sees that the others shrugged too, the theater starts to come apart. It starts small, in our own little worlds, but it grows into one more enormous collective NO. But that only works against nonsense and pretension. When it’s time to face other attacks and power grabs at every scale, what helps us is institutions.

A good part of our infrastructure and our institutions was built precisely to slow down, or even stop, what’s happening now. They’re being tested like never before. And so far, here in Brazil, they’ve held. It makes me so proud. And that’s exactly why so many modern right-wing projects involve weakening institutions, or seeing how far past the line they can get.

In that sense, Brazil remains a beacon of hope. Our 1988 Constitution. SUS, our public health system, an ambitious project that, imperfect as it is, covers an entire country of over 200 million, for free. The HIV drug patent we broke in 2007 in the public interest. Pix, our free public payment system, which shrugged at the banks and the card networks: the ones who charged to move our money around. The coup attempt of January 2023, which ended in the trial and imprisonment of the former president. The commanders of the Army and the Air Force who refused to support or assist that coup. The Supreme Court that stood up to Musk and made him back down, showing he needs our market more than we need him.

After all, our institutions, and above all the people inside them, are the third part of our leverage. We need to protect them and evolve them, so they can face a technology that advanced absurdly faster than they did.

With all our leverage, we need to take back social networks and AI. And as we do, we’ll need to watch for the greatest danger: handing the power to a new group. There’s no such thing as a group that’s good and powerful at the same time. Power corrupts. The goal isn’t to change owners; it’s to make the thing impossible for anyone to hold. Whoever offers to hold it for us is already on the side of power, not the people. We need to figure out how to hold all of this with no owner, only stewards — people who tend the structure — and do so transparently.

Maybe it’s pointing the surveillance apparatus the other way: up. Whoever works with a technology that, in their own words, could end humanity, agrees to be watched by the public. The eye turned only upward, with clear limits. Did business with whom? Went to which meeting? Changed which line of code? Took a bank transfer from whom? And so on.

Nothing in life is permanent. Institutions, democratic structures, our lives: everything becomes routine. And routines reveal loopholes and weak spots, so someone will always try to exploit again, in big or small ways. That’s fine, it’s part of it. The goal isn’t to break the cycle; it’s to keep it turning, with defenses that evolve along with it, like an immune system, guaranteeing a fairer baseline for the people. Breaking the cycle is what they want, with them on top. I accept the cycle. The cycle is life, progress, nature. And Brazilians know this well: it’s the ginga, the sway. Repeated, but always breaking the pattern.

In 1988, coming out of the dictatorship, we wrote a Constitution together, with popular amendments and all. I think we need something like that now, not a document, a pact: what we do for each other, how we protect our power, and what we don’t accept and don’t hand over. The first article is already written: nobody holds the key. The rest we write together. And not just us Brazilians: all of us who are fed up, the people of the world who want to uncouple from this theater headed somewhere dark, which is already taking from us without asking.

We have a lot to think through and work out together to set a better course, and to keep to it. We need every warrior we can get. Let’s do this?
